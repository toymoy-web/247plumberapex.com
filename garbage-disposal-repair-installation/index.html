Garbage Disposal Repair & Installation in Apex, NC

A jammed, leaking, or completely dead disposal gets diagnosed on site before deciding on a repair or a new unit.

A garbage disposal that's jammed, humming without spinning, or leaking from underneath the sink is one of the more common kitchen plumbing calls in Apex. Most jams are a simple mechanical fix. A unit that's leaking or has burned out its motor usually means replacement makes more sense than repair.

Disposals connect to both the plumbing and, in most installations, the dishwasher drain line, which means a leak or clog issue can sometimes involve more than just the disposal unit itself.

Why Disposals Fail

Jams are usually caused by fibrous foods, bones, or hard items that shouldn't go into a disposal in the first place, lodging between the spinning plate and the housing. A humming sound without spinning almost always means the motor is trying to turn against a jam rather than the motor itself having failed.

Leaks typically come from worn seals where the disposal connects to the sink flange or the dishwasher drain line, both serviceable connection points that degrade with age and use rather than necessarily meaning the whole unit has failed.

A disposal's average lifespan runs 8 to 12 years with normal use, and a unit approaching or past that range is a reasonable candidate for proactive replacement even before it fully fails, especially if it's already showing intermittent issues. Installation follows current Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) requirements as adopted under North Carolina's plumbing code.

Signs of a Failing Disposal

  • A humming sound with no spinning or grinding action
  • Water leaking from underneath the unit
  • A burning smell, often indicating a motor issue
  • The unit not responding at all to the switch
  • Unusual grinding noise even when nothing is inside

Diagnosing Jam vs. Failure

A humming unit gets checked for a mechanical jam first, often resolved directly or with the manual reset tool most disposals include, before assuming the motor itself has failed. A leak gets traced to its specific connection point, the sink flange, the dishwasher line, or the disposal body itself, since only a body leak typically requires full replacement.

A unit that's completely unresponsive, no humming, no sound at all, gets checked for power supply and the reset button before assuming the disposal itself is dead.

Repair vs. Replacement

Jammed but motor sound present

Manual unjamming, usually a same-visit fix.

Leaking at sink flange or dishwasher line

Seal replacement at the specific connection point.

Leaking from the disposal body itself

Full replacement. Body leaks aren't practically repairable.

Burned-out motor or no power response

Replacement, since motor failure isn't a repairable issue.

Disposal Just Humming?

Usually a simple jam, not a dead motor.

What to Do Before We Arrive

  • Switch off the disposal at the wall switch if it's making unusual noise or smells like burning.
  • Never reach into a disposal by hand, even when it's off, to clear a jam.
  • Shut off power at the breaker if the unit won't respond to the switch at all.

Cost and What's Included

Unjamming and seal repairs are among the more affordable kitchen plumbing fixes, often completed in a single visit. A full replacement costs more but includes removing the old unit, connecting the new one to existing plumbing and electrical, and testing it before the job is complete.

A replacement is also a reasonable time to consider a more powerful unit if the household regularly deals with jams on the current model, since motor horsepower varies meaningfully between disposal models.

Electrical connections for a hardwired disposal are verified as part of any installation, since improper wiring is both a safety issue and a common cause of a unit that won't respond at all.
