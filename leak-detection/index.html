Leak Detection in Apex, NC

A rising water bill, a damp spot with no obvious source, or a meter that keeps running with everything off all point to a leak that hasn't shown itself yet.

Most leak detection calls in Apex, NC start the same way: a water bill that jumped for no clear reason, or a damp patch on a wall, ceiling, or floor with nothing obviously wrong nearby. The water is going somewhere. Finding exactly where, without tearing open walls or digging up a yard on a guess, is what leak detection equipment is built for.

A hidden leak behind drywall, under a slab, or along a buried supply line doesn't announce itself the way a burst pipe does. It shows up as a symptom somewhere else, and connecting that symptom to its real source is the actual job here.

Where Hidden Leaks Usually Come From in Apex

Older Apex neighborhoods with original copper or galvanized supply lines see more pinhole leaks as pipe walls thin from decades of use. Wake County's clay-heavy soil also shifts with moisture, which puts steady stress on buried lines regardless of a home's age.

A water heater tank starting to fail can also leak slowly for weeks before it's obvious, often showing up first as a damp spot near the base of the unit or a slightly higher water bill rather than a dramatic failure.

Signs of a Hidden Leak

  • A water bill that's noticeably higher with no change in usage
  • The water meter keeps moving with every fixture in the house off
  • A damp or discolored patch on a wall, ceiling, or floor
  • A musty smell without visible water
  • A warm spot on a slab floor (often a hot water line leak)

How the Leak Actually Gets Found

Acoustic leak detection equipment listens for the sound water makes escaping under pressure, which can pinpoint a leak's location through a wall or slab without opening it up first. Pressure testing on isolated sections of the plumbing system narrows down which line is affected before any acoustic work even starts.

For a suspected slab leak specifically, thermal imaging sometimes supplements acoustic detection, since a hot water line leak under a slab often creates a detectable warm spot on the floor above it.

What Happens Once the Leak Is Found

Accessible pipe

A leak behind an accessible wall or under a sink is usually a straightforward repair.

Slab leak

Confirmed leaks under a slab typically need a spot repair through a small access point, not breaking up the whole floor.

Buried supply line

A leak in the yard between the meter and the house may need a section of pipe replaced or rerouted.

Water heater tank

A leak from the tank body itself means replacement, not repair.

What It Costs

Leak detection pricing reflects the diagnostic work itself: acoustic equipment, pressure testing, and technician time to narrow down a location before any repair estimate is given. A confirmed leak's repair cost then depends entirely on where it is and how accessible it is.

Homeowners insurance often covers the resulting damage from a leak but rarely covers the detection or repair of the leak itself, so it's worth confirming with a specific policy before assuming coverage. Any repair identified follows current Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) requirements as adopted under North Carolina's plumbing code.
