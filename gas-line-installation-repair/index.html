Gas Line Installation & Repair in Apex, NC

Gas line work in Apex, NC covers two different situations: running a new line to a gas appliance, a range, dryer, tankless water heater, or outdoor fire feature, and repairing or inspecting an existing line that's showing a problem. Both require the same code compliance and pressure testing regardless of scope.

A gas leak specifically is a safety issue, not a standard repair call. Any suspected leak, indicated by a sulfur or rotten-egg smell, gets treated with immediate urgency rather than scheduled like routine work.

Older Apex homes converted from oil or electric heat to gas over the years sometimes have line sizing that no longer matches current appliance demand, worth checking during any new installation on an older property.

Common Gas Line Situations

New installations most often come from a kitchen renovation adding a gas range, a switch from electric to gas for a water heater or dryer, or an outdoor kitchen or fire feature addition. Each requires sizing the line correctly for the appliance's BTU demand.

Repairs on existing lines are less common but do happen, usually from corrosion on an older line or physical damage from unrelated construction or landscaping work nearby.

When to Call Immediately

  • A sulfur or rotten-egg smell anywhere near a gas appliance or line
  • A hissing sound near a gas connection
  • A pilot light that won't stay lit despite a working thermocouple
  • Visible corrosion or damage on an exposed gas line
  • Dead vegetation in a patch above a buried gas line

How Gas Line Work Is Verified

Every new gas line installation is pressure tested before an appliance is connected, confirming there are no leaks at any joint or fitting before the line goes into use. This isn't optional. It's standard practice regardless of how confident the installation looked.

For a suspected leak on an existing line, a gas leak detector and soap solution at every joint locates the exact point before any repair, since guessing at a fix on a gas line is not an acceptable approach.

Installation vs. Repair Scope

New appliance connection

Line sized to the appliance's BTU rating, run and pressure tested.

Existing line, suspected leak

Located with a gas detector, then repaired or replaced at that point.

Old, uninspected line

A full inspection before connecting any new appliance to it.

Outdoor fixture addition

A dedicated line run and tested for the specific fixture's demand.

Smell Gas? Don't Wait.

Leave the area and call immediately. This isn't a scheduled repair.

Cost and Code Compliance

Cost depends on the line's length, whether it's a new run or a repair, and whether it requires a permit or inspection under current North Carolina code. All gas line work follows the plumbing code adopted by the state, verified and pressure-tested before any appliance is connected.

A permit is typically required for new gas line installations. That process and its timeline get explained upfront rather than skipped to save time.

For any repair on an existing line, the affected section is isolated and the gas supply shut off before work begins, and the full line is re-tested afterward before service is restored to confirm the repair holds under normal operating pressure.
