A clogged drain that backs up every few weeks despite repeated snaking usually isn't a snaking problem anymore. In Apex, NC, that pattern points to either root intrusion into an older clay or cast-iron line, common in established neighborhoods, or a partial collapse from the Piedmont region's shifting clay soil putting pressure on a buried pipe.

A single clogged sink or tub, on the other hand, is almost always a local blockage close to the fixture and clears with standard cabling in one visit.

What's Actually Behind a Recurring Clog

Grease and soap buildup narrows kitchen and bathroom lines gradually until normal flow triggers a backup. Root intrusion is the more serious version: tree roots find a hairline crack or joint in an older sewer line and grow inward, and simple snaking often just punches a hole through the root mass without clearing it, which is why the clog returns within weeks.

New-construction homes near Green Level see fewer root-related clogs but more issues from construction debris left in lines during the building process.

When It's More Than a Simple Clog

  • The same drain clogs again within a few weeks of being cleared
  • Multiple fixtures back up at the same time (a sign of a main line issue)
  • Gurgling sounds from other drains when one is running
  • Water backing up into a lower fixture, like a tub, when using another
  • A slow drain that's gradually gotten worse over months

How the Real Cause Gets Found

A drain camera is run through the line for any recurring clog before recommending a fix, rather than snaking the same line a third time and hoping for a different result. The camera shows whether the problem is grease buildup, root intrusion, a belly (a low spot where the pipe has settled), or a cracked joint.

Where roots or heavy buildup are confirmed, hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scour the full diameter of the pipe rather than punching a single channel through the blockage the way a cable does.

Snaking vs. Hydro Jetting vs. Repair

Single clog, first occurrence

Standard cabling/snaking usually resolves it in one visit.

Recurring clog, same line

Camera inspection first, then hydro jetting if grease or minor roots are confirmed.

Root intrusion, cracked pipe

Hydro jetting clears it temporarily. A cracked line still needs a spot repair to actually stop the recurrence.

Multiple fixtures backing up

Main line issue, evaluated as a sewer line repair case, not simple unclogging.

What It Costs and What's Included

Standard cabling for a single clogged fixture costs less than a camera inspection plus hydro jetting, since the second option involves more diagnostic time and equipment. The actual price is confirmed after seeing the line, not quoted blind over the phone.

A clog that's actually a symptom of a cracked or collapsed line will keep costing money to re-clear until the underlying pipe issue is fixed, which is why a recurring clog gets a camera inspection instead of a third round of snaking.

Kitchen drains carry a different failure pattern than bathroom drains. Grease and food particles build up gradually along the inside of the pipe wall in a kitchen line, narrowing it over months, while a bathroom clog is more often hair and soap scum concentrated at a single point near a trap. That difference is part of why a kitchen line that clogs repeatedly is a stronger candidate for hydro jetting than a first-time bathroom clog.
