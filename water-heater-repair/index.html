No hot water on a cold morning is one of the more common emergency calls in Apex, NC, and the cause is rarely obvious from the symptom alone. A gas unit with no hot water at all often points to a pilot light or thermocouple issue. an electric unit is more often a failed heating element or tripped thermostat.

A leaking fitting is a different repair entirely from a leaking tank. Fittings, valves, and connections can usually be repaired in place. A tank that's leaking from the tank itself, rather than a connection point, generally cannot be repaired and needs replacement instead.

Why Water Heaters Fail in Apex Homes

Harder municipal water through Town of Apex Water Resources accelerates sediment buildup at the bottom of the tank, which makes the heating element or burner work harder than it should and shortens component life. This shows up most in established neighborhoods with tanks that haven't been flushed regularly.

Thermostats, heating elements, and gas valves are also simply wear parts with a limited lifespan regardless of water hardness, and a unit that's never had a single repair by year 8 or 9 is statistically due for one.

Signs of a Repairable Problem

  • No hot water, but the tank itself isn't leaking
  • Lukewarm water or hot water that runs out faster than it used to
  • A pilot light that won't stay lit (gas units)
  • Water heater making popping or rumbling noises
  • A leak at a fitting or valve, not the tank body

How the Diagnosis Works

The tank is checked first for any sign of a leak from the tank body itself, since that single check decides whether this is a repair call or a replacement conversation. If the tank is sound, the thermostat, heating element (electric) or thermocouple and gas valve (gas) get tested directly rather than replaced on a guess.

A flush is often recommended alongside a repair when sediment buildup is visible in the tank, since skipping it just means the same component fails again sooner from the same cause. Any gas connection work follows current Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) requirements as adopted under North Carolina's plumbing code, not a shortcut fix.

What Gets Fixed and What Doesn't

Thermostat or heating element

Repairable in most cases, usually same-visit with the part in stock.

Pilot light or thermocouple

Repairable. A recurring pilot outage sometimes points to a venting issue worth checking.

Leaking fitting or valve

Repairable in place without replacing the tank.

Leaking tank body

Not repairable. This is a replacement, not a repair, situation.

Before Help Arrives

  • Switch off the water heater's gas supply or breaker if it's actively leaking.
  • Don't relight a gas pilot yourself if it smells like gas. Shut off the gas supply and call.
  • Shut off the cold-water supply valve on top of the tank if it's leaking from a fitting.
  • Keep the area around the unit clear of stored items in case of further leaking.

Cost and What's Included

A repair visit includes diagnosing the actual cause before quoting a fix, so the price reflects the real part and labor involved rather than a flat guess. Common parts like thermostats and heating elements are typically stocked on the truck. Specialty parts for older or less common units can add a return trip.

If the diagnosis turns up a leaking tank rather than a repairable component, that gets explained clearly before any work is approved, including what a replacement would involve instead.

Gas and electric units fail differently enough that the repair itself looks different too. A gas unit with a pilot that won't hold usually points to a thermocouple that's no longer generating enough voltage to keep the gas valve open. An electric unit that trips its breaker repeatedly often means a shorted heating element rather than a simple reset-and-hope fix.
