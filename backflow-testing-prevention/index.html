Backflow Testing & Prevention in Apex, NC

Backflow prevention devices protect the municipal water supply from contamination and typically require annual testing to stay compliant.

A backflow prevention device stops water from flowing backward into the municipal supply, which matters most on properties with irrigation systems, fire suppression lines, or certain commercial connections where cross-contamination risk is higher than a typical single-family home.

Many of these devices require annual testing to confirm they're functioning correctly, often a requirement from Town of Apex Water Resources or tied to a property's specific water service agreement.

Why Backflow Prevention Matters

A backflow event happens when a drop in municipal water pressure, from firefighting activity, a major water main break, or heavy demand, creates a vacuum that can pull contaminated water backward from a property's irrigation, fire suppression, or plumbing system into the public supply. A functioning prevention device stops that from happening.

Irrigation systems are one of the more common residential backflow concerns in Apex, since a sprinkler system connects directly to the water supply and often has fertilizer or treated water present in its lines at various points.

Commercial properties face additional scrutiny, since fire suppression systems, boiler feed lines, and certain industrial connections carry higher contamination risk than a typical residential hookup, which is part of why utilities and code enforcement track commercial backflow compliance closely.

Even a small residential irrigation system technically creates a cross-connection risk, which is why many municipalities, including requirements tied to Town of Apex Water Resources, require a device on any property with an irrigation hookup regardless of its size.

Who Typically Needs Testing

  • Any property with an irrigation or sprinkler system
  • Commercial properties with fire suppression systems
  • Properties with a specific backflow device already installed
  • New construction with an irrigation system being connected
  • Any property notified by the utility that testing is due

How Testing Works

A certified test checks the prevention device's internal check valves and relief mechanism under controlled pressure conditions, confirming the device actually stops backward flow rather than assuming it does based on age or appearance.

Results are documented and typically submitted to the water utility as proof of compliance, which is often the actual requirement driving the test rather than a voluntary safety check alone.

The test itself is quick, usually well under an hour for a standard residential device, and doesn't require shutting off water to the rest of the property during the process, which keeps disruption minimal.

What to Do Before Testing

  • Confirm the device's location is accessible and not blocked by landscaping or storage.
  • Note any recent low-pressure events or water main work in the area, which is useful context for the technician.
  • Keep any prior test documentation on hand if this isn't the first test on the device.

Cost and Documentation

Annual testing is a relatively low-cost, quick service compared to the cost of a compliance issue with the utility. Documentation is provided directly, and submission to Town of Apex Water Resources is handled as part of the service where required.

A device that fails testing gets repaired or replaced promptly, since an unresolved failed test can affect a property's water service status with the utility.
