Freeze Prep for Pipes in the Piedmont's Occasional Hard Freezes

By the Apex 24/7 Plumbing team · 2026-09-15

Have this exact problem right now? Skip the reading and call (919) 944-7281 directly.

Apex doesn't deal with the kind of sustained winter cold that makes freeze protection a daily concern the way it is farther north. That's exactly what makes the occasional hard freeze here more dangerous to plumbing, not less. Homes and pipe layouts in the Piedmont simply weren't built with the same freeze-protection assumptions as homes in consistently cold climates.

Why Mild Climates Get Caught Off Guard

In regions with consistent winter cold, pipes are routinely insulated, buried below the frost line, and routed away from exterior walls as standard practice. In Apex, where hard freezes are the exception rather than the rule, exposed pipes in crawlspaces, garages, and exterior walls are far more common, simply because freeze protection hasn't historically needed to be a top priority.

When a genuine hard freeze does arrive, usually a sudden, short-duration cold snap rather than a sustained winter pattern, those same pipes are exactly the ones most likely to crack.

Which Pipes Are Actually at Risk

  • Pipes running through an unheated crawlspace
  • Pipes along exterior walls with minimal insulation
  • Pipes in a garage, especially near the garage door
  • Outdoor hose bibs and any attached supply line
  • Pipes in an attic space above living areas

What to Do Before a Freeze Warning

  • Disconnect and drain outdoor hoses, and shut off any exterior hose bib valve if one exists
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls to let warm air reach the pipes
  • Let faucets drip slightly during the coldest hours to keep water moving
  • Know where the main water shutoff valve is, in case a pipe does fail
  • Add pipe insulation sleeves to any exposed pipes in a crawlspace or garage

What to Do If a Pipe Freezes But Hasn't Burst Yet

A frozen pipe that hasn't yet cracked can sometimes be safely thawed, using a hair dryer or warm towels applied gently along the pipe, never an open flame. Signs of a frozen but intact pipe include reduced or no water flow at a fixture without any other explanation, especially right after a cold night.

If there's any sign of bulging, cracking, or the pipe has already started leaking, stop attempting to thaw it and shut off the water at the main valve instead.

Why a Cracked Pipe Doesn't Always Leak Immediately

A pipe can crack while still frozen without any visible leak at all, since the ice itself is temporarily plugging the crack. The leak often doesn't show up until the pipe thaws and water starts flowing through the now-damaged section, which is why damage sometimes seems to appear well after the cold snap has already passed.

One-Time Fixes vs. Ongoing Vulnerability

A home that's experienced a frozen or burst pipe once is worth evaluating for a permanent fix, better insulation, rerouting an exposed section, or adding heat tape along a vulnerable run, rather than just repairing the immediate damage and hoping the next freeze doesn't repeat the problem.

This is especially worth considering for older homes in established Apex neighborhoods where original construction predates more modern freeze-protection standards.

How Cold Does It Actually Need to Get?

Pipe freezing risk generally starts becoming real once outdoor temperatures drop into the low 20s Fahrenheit for a sustained period, particularly overnight when there's no solar warming to offset the cold. A single overnight dip isn't usually enough to freeze a well-insulated interior pipe, but an exposed pipe in a crawlspace or garage can be at risk even from a shorter cold snap.

Wind is an underappreciated factor too. A cold, still night is less risky than a colder night with sustained wind hitting an exterior wall where pipes run close to the surface, since wind chill accelerates heat loss through building materials faster than still cold air does.

Crawlspace Vents: Open or Closed?

Many older Apex homes have crawlspace vents that stay open year-round for moisture control. During a hard freeze forecast, temporarily closing these vents helps retain whatever warmth exists in the crawlspace and reduces the direct cold-air exposure to any pipes running through it.

This is a temporary measure for freeze events specifically, not a permanent change, since crawlspace ventilation serves an important moisture-control purpose the rest of the year that shouldn't be disrupted long-term.

What About Homes With a Well or Irrigation System

Irrigation systems need special attention before a freeze, since standing water in exposed above-ground components, particularly backflow prevention devices, can freeze and crack housing that's expensive to replace. Draining or insulating these components ahead of a forecasted freeze is worth the effort given the cost of replacing a cracked backflow device compared to a few minutes of prep.

Homes on a well face similar concerns for any exposed wellhead components, which is worth checking specifically if a hard freeze is forecast and the wellhead isn't already insulated or housed.

Insurance Considerations for Freeze Damage

Homeowners insurance typically covers sudden freeze-related pipe bursts, treating them similarly to any other sudden, accidental water damage. Some policies include specific language requiring reasonable freeze prevention steps, like maintaining heat in the home during cold weather, so leaving a home unheated during an extended absence in freezing conditions can complicate a claim if a pipe does fail.

For anyone traveling during winter, keeping the thermostat set to a reasonable minimum temperature, rather than turning heat off entirely, is both practical freeze prevention and often a genuine policy requirement worth confirming directly.

A brief check-in with a neighbor or a smart thermostat alert set up before an extended winter trip gives an early warning if indoor temperatures drop further than expected, catching a developing problem before it turns into a frozen or burst pipe.

Recovering From a Freeze Event

After a hard freeze passes, it's worth checking every fixture in the house, running water briefly at each one and watching for any drop in pressure, discoloration, or unexpected sounds. A pipe that survived the freeze without cracking can still have suffered stress that shows up as a slow leak days or even weeks later, so a follow-up check a week or two after a significant cold event is a reasonable precaution.

Checking the crawlspace or garage directly, if either is accessible, for any sign of dripping or dampness gives a more direct read than relying solely on fixture behavior inside the house, especially in the days right after a cold snap passes.

If a pipe has already burst during a freeze, this gets treated as an emergency: a burst pipe repair visit prioritizes stopping the water and fixing the immediate damage, and where flooding has already occurred, restoration work addresses that directly. Older homes in Haddon Hall with original copper supply lines are a common example of exactly this vulnerability during a hard freeze, especially in sections closest to the community's original 1990s construction phase.
