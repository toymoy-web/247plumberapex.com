A First-Time Homeowner's Plumbing Checklist for Apex, NC

By the Apex 24/7 Plumbing team · 2026-09-15

Have this exact problem right now? Skip the reading and call (919) 944-7281 directly.

Moving from renting to owning a home changes the plumbing conversation entirely. A running toilet or a slow drain used to be a call to a landlord. Now it's a decision that actually belongs to you, along with a handful of basics worth knowing before something goes wrong rather than during an emergency.

Know These Four Things Immediately

  • Where the main water shutoff valve is located
  • Where the water heater is and roughly how old it is
  • Whether the home is on municipal sewer or a septic system
  • Which ZIP code and utility, Town of Apex Water Resources or otherwise, serves the property

Why the Shutoff Valve Comes First

Every plumbing emergency scenario, a burst pipe, a failed water heater, a supply line disconnecting, gets dramatically less damaging if the water gets shut off quickly. Finding and testing this valve on a calm day, rather than searching for it during an actual emergency, is the single highest-value five minutes a new homeowner can spend.

Understanding Your Home's Age and Materials

A home's approximate build year tells you a lot about what to expect. Homes from before the 1990s in established Apex neighborhoods may still have original galvanized supply lines, which age differently than the PEX found in newer construction. Knowing which category a home falls into helps set realistic expectations for what maintenance and eventual repairs might look like. Any repair work performed today follows current Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) requirements as adopted under North Carolina's plumbing code, regardless of which code version applied when the home was originally built.

A Simple First-Year Maintenance Calendar

  • Flush the water heater once, ideally in the first few months, to establish a baseline
  • Test every toilet in the house with the food coloring flapper test
  • Locate and test the main shutoff valve
  • If there's a sump pump, test it with a bucket of water
  • Before the first hard freeze, insulate any exposed pipes in a crawlspace or garage

Getting a Baseline Inspection

For a resale home, especially an older one, a general plumbing inspection shortly after moving in establishes a clear baseline: what's working fine, what's aging but not urgent, and what genuinely needs attention soon. This is different from the inspection done during the home purchase itself, which is often more limited in scope than a dedicated plumbing review.

Having this baseline makes every future conversation with a plumber faster and more accurate, since there's already a documented starting point rather than guessing at a system's unknown history.

Learning Your Home's Water Bill Pattern

Reviewing the first few months of water bills after moving in establishes a baseline for normal usage, which makes it far easier to notice a genuine anomaly later, a sudden spike that might indicate a hidden leak, for example. Without a baseline, a rising bill is much harder to interpret correctly.

This is a simple, five-minute task each month that pays off considerably if it ever catches a leak early instead of months into an unexplained bill increase.

Knowing What Not to Put Down Drains

Grease, coffee grounds, fibrous vegetable scraps, and so-called flushable wipes are common causes of clogs that many new homeowners don't realize until after a backup happens. Establishing good habits from the start, a countertop container for grease rather than the sink, for example, prevents a meaningful share of avoidable service calls down the road.

Setting Realistic Expectations for an Older Home

If the home is decades old, it's worth accepting upfront that some plumbing repairs are simply part of ownership over the coming years, not a sign that something was hidden during the purchase. Budgeting mentally, and financially, for periodic plumbing maintenance from the start makes it feel like normal homeownership rather than a series of unwelcome surprises.

Keeping Records From Day One

Starting a simple folder, physical or digital, for every plumbing-related receipt, inspection report, and repair record from the very first year of ownership pays off considerably down the line. This documentation helps with warranty claims, future repair conversations, and eventually with resale, since a well-documented maintenance history is genuinely valuable to a future buyer.

It's a small habit to build early, and it's far easier to start on day one than to try to reconstruct years of history later when it's actually needed.

Don't Wait for a Crisis to Learn the Basics

Every item on this list is easier to handle calmly before there's an actual problem than during one. A first-time homeowner who spends a single afternoon working through this checklist is meaningfully better prepared than one who learns everything reactively, one emergency at a time.

It Gets Easier After the First Year

The first year of homeownership involves the steepest learning curve, simply because everything is new and unfamiliar. Once the basics from this checklist are handled, subsequent years involve mostly routine, predictable maintenance rather than a constant stream of unknowns, which makes the upfront effort well worth it.

Where to Go From Here

None of this needs to happen in a single weekend. Spreading these tasks across the first few months of ownership, shutoff valve first, then the water heater flush, then the toilet checks, keeps it manageable rather than overwhelming. The goal is simply to reach a point where the home's basic plumbing systems and history are familiar rather than a complete unknown, one task checked off at a time.

From there, ordinary maintenance and the occasional repair become routine parts of homeownership rather than sources of stress, which is really the whole point of building this foundation early.

A little effort in the first few months pays off for years, turning what could be a stressful string of surprises into an ordinary, manageable part of owning a home in Apex for the years ahead, rather than a source of ongoing stress.

Building a Relationship With a Plumber Before You Need One

Many new homeowners only think about calling a plumber during an actual emergency, which means making that first call under stress with no prior context. Having a general inspection or even a small maintenance visit done early establishes that context ahead of time, which makes any future emergency call faster and more informed on both sides.

This relationship doesn't need to be formal. Even a single prior visit for something minor gives a plumber useful context about the home's layout, age, and any quirks worth remembering for next time, which speeds up every conversation after that first one, saving time for both the homeowner and the plumber on every visit that follows.

If you've recently bought a home in Apex and want a real baseline on its plumbing condition, a general plumbing inspection covers exactly that, and if the home turns out to be on a septic system, septic evaluation is worth adding to that same visit. This is a common first step for new residents throughout Salem and other newer Apex neighborhoods.
