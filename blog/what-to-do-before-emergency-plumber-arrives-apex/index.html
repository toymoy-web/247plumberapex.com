What to Do Before an Emergency Plumber Arrives

By the Apex 24/7 Plumbing team · 2026-09-15

Have this exact problem right now? Skip the reading and call (919) 944-7281 directly.

The minutes right after discovering a plumbing emergency matter more than people realize. What happens before the plumber arrives, not just the repair itself, often determines how much damage a burst pipe, backup, or leak actually causes.

For a Burst Pipe or Active Leak

  • Shut off the main water valve immediately if it's accessible and safe to reach
  • Move furniture, electronics, and valuables away from the water
  • Switch off electricity to the affected area at the breaker if water is near outlets
  • Photograph the damage before any cleanup begins, for insurance purposes

For a Sewage Backup

  • Avoid any direct contact with the backed-up sewage
  • Don't run water, flush toilets, or use the dishwasher or washing machine
  • Ventilate the area if there's a noticeable odor
  • Keep children and pets away from the affected area entirely

For No Hot Water or No Water at All

This is less urgent than an active leak but still worth acting on. Check whether a shutoff valve near the water heater or main line has been accidentally closed, which is a surprisingly common cause that doesn't require any repair at all. If everything appears open and there's still no water, that's worth reporting clearly when calling.

If You Smell Gas

This is different from every other scenario. Leave the house immediately, don't operate any light switches, thermostats, or electrical devices on the way out, and call from outside once you're safely away from the property. This is treated as an emergency requiring immediate attention, not a standard plumbing call.

What to Have Ready When You Call

  • The exact location of the problem within the house
  • Whether water is actively flowing or has been stopped
  • Your address and the best callback number
  • Whether the main water valve has already been shut off

Where the Main Water Shutoff Usually Is

In most Apex homes, the main shutoff valve is located where the water line enters the house, often in a basement, crawlspace access area, garage, or utility closet. Finding and testing this valve before an emergency happens, rather than searching for it during one, saves critical time when it actually matters.

A valve that's rarely used can also seize up over time, so testing it periodically, a quarter turn and back, keeps it functional for the moment it's actually needed.

What Actually Counts as an Emergency

Not every plumbing problem needs an after-hours call. Active flooding, sewage backup, no water at all, or a suspected gas leak are genuine emergencies. A slow drain, a minor drip, or a toilet that's running but not overflowing can usually wait for standard business hours without causing additional damage in the meantime.

Knowing this distinction helps avoid unnecessary after-hours costs for situations that aren't actually urgent, while still making sure genuine emergencies get the priority response they need.

Documenting Damage as It Happens

Taking photos and, where safe, brief video of active damage before cleanup begins makes an insurance claim considerably easier to process later. This doesn't need to delay getting the water shut off or calling for help. It only takes a minute once the immediate danger is addressed.

Noting the approximate time the problem was first noticed is also worth writing down, since insurance claims sometimes ask for this detail and it's easy to forget the exact timing once the situation is resolved.

After the Immediate Crisis Passes

Once water is shut off and immediate safety steps are taken, avoid running any appliances or fixtures connected to the affected area until a plumber has assessed the situation. This prevents accidentally making the damage worse or creating a new problem while waiting for help to arrive.

Preparing Your Home Before Anything Goes Wrong

Beyond finding the main shutoff valve, it helps to know where individual fixture shutoff valves are, under each sink and behind each toilet, since a localized problem sometimes only needs that specific valve closed rather than shutting off water to the entire house. This limits inconvenience for a problem that doesn't require a whole-house response.

Keeping a small emergency kit, a flashlight, a few towels, and a bucket, in an easily accessible spot near the main shutoff valve means these basic tools are ready when needed rather than requiring a search during an actual crisis.

Talking to Household Members About the Plan

Everyone in the household, not just whoever usually handles home maintenance, benefits from knowing where the main shutoff valve is and what the basic emergency steps are. A plumbing emergency doesn't wait for the one person who knows these details to be home, and having everyone capable of taking the first response step can make a real, measurable difference in how much damage occurs.

A Short List Worth Keeping Visible

Printing or writing out the main shutoff valve location, the emergency phone number, and the basic steps for each scenario covered here, then keeping that list somewhere visible, inside a utility closet door or on the refrigerator, removes the need to remember any of this from memory during an actual crisis. Under stress, a visible reference is far more reliable than trying to recall the right steps.

Renters vs. Homeowners: The Steps Still Apply

Renters facing a plumbing emergency should still know these same basic steps, shutting off water, avoiding electrical hazards, documenting damage, even though the repair itself and any cost responsibility falls to the landlord. Contacting the landlord or property manager promptly after taking immediate safety steps is the renter's equivalent of calling a plumber directly.

Knowing the shutoff valve location in a rental is just as valuable as in an owned home, since the immediate damage prevention steps don't change based on who owns the property, only who pays for the eventual repair.

Multi-Unit and HOA Properties Add a Step

In a townhome or condo with shared walls, an emergency sometimes affects a neighboring unit too, particularly a leak that travels through a shared wall or floor. Notifying an affected neighbor promptly, alongside taking the same shutoff and safety steps, helps limit the damage on both sides and avoids a delayed discovery that makes the eventual cleanup more extensive than it needed to be.

Staying Calm Helps More Than It Seems

Most plumbing emergencies feel more catastrophic in the moment than the actual repair scope usually turns out to be. A calm, clear description of what's happening when calling for help gets a plumber the right information faster than a panicked, disorganized account of the situation, which genuinely does affect how quickly the right response gets dispatched.

Whatever the specific emergency, the emergency line runs 24/7 for exactly these situations, and if the immediate cause is a failed pipe specifically, burst pipe repair gets prioritized ahead of routine calls. Knowing these steps ahead of time is especially useful in Scott's Mill and other creekside neighborhoods, where slower drainage after heavy rain means water damage can escalate faster than in higher, faster-draining parts of Apex.
