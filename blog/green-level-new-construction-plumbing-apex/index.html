New Construction in Green Level: What to Know About Your Home's Plumbing

By the Apex 24/7 Plumbing team · 2026-09-15

Have this exact problem right now? Skip the reading and call (919) 944-7281 directly.

Green Level has become one of the fastest-growing corridors in Apex, with new construction from builders like M/I Homes filling in around the area's historic crossroads along Green Level West Road. For buyers moving into a brand-new home here, the plumbing questions look different than they would for an established, decades-old Apex property.

New Doesn't Mean No Questions

A new-construction home comes with modern PEX supply lines and current code compliance, which eliminates the corrosion and outdated-material concerns that dominate conversations about older Apex homes. That doesn't mean there's nothing worth checking. New construction has its own, different set of common issues, and all of it is still verified against current Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) requirements as adopted under North Carolina's plumbing code.

What to Check Before Move-In

  • Water pressure at multiple fixtures simultaneously, testing for undersized supply lines
  • Confirm the water heater is sized appropriately for the home's actual bathroom count
  • Check for any visible connections that seem rushed or incomplete near the water heater or main shutoff
  • Test all shutoff valves individually to confirm they actually function
  • Ask whether backflow prevention was installed if the home has irrigation

Water Heater Sizing Is a Common Builder Shortcut

Builders sometimes install a water heater sized to meet minimum code requirements rather than to comfortably serve a home's actual bathroom count and expected household size. This shows up after move-in as running out of hot water during normal morning routines, which feels like a defect but is really an undersizing decision made during original construction.

Checking the installed unit's capacity against the home's actual bathroom count before or shortly after move-in avoids an unpleasant surprise a few weeks in.

Irrigation and Backflow: An Easy Item to Miss

New construction with a sprinkler system installed as part of the original build should include a backflow prevention device by default, but it's worth confirming directly rather than assuming. Missing this component means dealing with a retrofit and a compliance gap that shouldn't have existed from day one on a brand-new installation.

Asking specifically whether backflow testing has already been scheduled for the first year is a reasonable question during the closing process, since the requirement applies regardless of how new the system is.

Fixture Quality Varies More Than Expected

Builder-grade fixtures, faucets, valves, and toilets included in a base new-construction package, vary considerably in quality between builders and even between different price tiers from the same builder. Some perform well for years. Others show wear and minor issues within the first year or two, which is part of why builder warranty coverage matters as much as it does for new construction specifically.

Testing every fixture thoroughly during a final walkthrough, not just checking that water comes out, helps catch a genuinely weak fixture while it's still covered rather than after the warranty window closes.

Understanding the Historic Crossroads Side

For buyers looking at the older homes near Green Level's historic 1800s crossroads rather than new construction, the plumbing considerations flip entirely: original galvanized or early copper lines, older fixture styles, and potentially a septic system rather than municipal sewer connection. These homes deserve the same due diligence given to any older Apex property, regardless of the surrounding area's overall growth and new-construction reputation.

It's worth confirming directly which category a specific property falls into rather than assuming based on the surrounding neighborhood's general growth trend.

Coordinating With Builders on Multiple Units

For buyers purchasing in a phase still under active construction, coordinating inspection timing with the builder's own schedule helps ensure plumbing systems are fully tested before final walkthrough rather than rushed through at the last minute. This is worth raising directly with a builder's representative during the closing process.

What to Do With a Long List of Small Issues

It's common for a final walkthrough to surface a handful of minor plumbing items, a slightly loose fixture, a fill valve that runs a bit long, a faucet with slightly uneven flow. None of these individually warrant panic, but documenting all of them together in a single punch list, rather than reporting them piecemeal as they're each noticed, gives the builder a clear, complete picture to address before the warranty window closes.

The Growth Corridor Will Keep Bringing Questions

As Green Level continues developing along the NC-540 and Highway 64 corridor, new residents will keep facing this same set of new-construction questions for years to come. Sharing what's been learned, whether informally with neighbors or through community resources, helps the next wave of buyers navigate the same builder warranty windows and sizing questions with less friction than the first residents faced in the corridor's earliest phases.

This steady influx of new-construction questions also means there's real, current local experience with these specific builders and their common installation patterns, which is worth drawing on rather than treating every new-construction plumbing question as unprecedented.

Whether the home is brand new or decades old, the underlying principle stays the same: know what's actually installed, test it before assuming it works, and address small issues while they're still small rather than letting them compound into something more expensive to deal with down the road.

The Builder Warranty Window

Most new construction comes with a limited warranty covering plumbing defects for a set period, often one year, sometimes longer for specific systems. Any issue discovered during this window is worth reporting to the builder directly first, since a genuine installation defect should be covered rather than paid for out of pocket.

Keeping a simple log of any plumbing issues noticed during the first year, even minor ones, creates a useful record if a warranty claim becomes necessary later.

New Construction Doesn't Mean Maintenance-Free

It's easy to assume a brand-new home needs no plumbing attention for years. In reality, Apex's harder municipal water starts affecting even new fixtures and a new water heater from day one, the same way it does anywhere else in the service area. Starting a regular maintenance routine early, rather than waiting for the home to feel old, actually extends the lifespan of new systems more than skipping maintenance because everything is new.

The Split Character of Green Level Itself

New Green Level construction sits alongside a genuinely older population of homes near the historic crossroads, which means the neighborhood as a whole sees both new-construction inspection calls and older-home repair calls, sometimes on the same street. This mix is part of what makes Green Level distinct among Apex's growth areas.

If you've just moved into new construction in Green Level, a water heater sizing check is worth doing early rather than waiting for a shortage to reveal it, and for any builder warranty issue involving a fixture or connection, a general plumbing inspection documents the issue clearly for a warranty claim. This comes up often for buyers throughout the Green Level growth corridor.
