About Downtown Apex

Downtown Apex is the oldest part of town. Salem Street runs through the middle of it, lined with one- and two-story brick buildings, and the Apex Historic District around it was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 1994. The listing was expanded in 1995, 2002 and 2008, and the district reaches out to Hunter, Center, Chatham, Cunningham, Holleman and Hughes Streets.

Apex was incorporated in 1873, and the buildings that survive in the district date from about 1870 to 1940. Fires in 1905 and 1911 destroyed much of the early downtown, and owners rebuilt in brick. Today the Apex Union Depot and the Town Hall sit on Salem Street, and the Halle Cultural Arts Center is just off it.

Around the commercial blocks are older residential streets with cottages, bungalows and renovated homes, usually on smaller lots than the planned subdivisions elsewhere in Apex. Sidewalks are common, and many residents walk to dinner or to events downtown.

Plumbing in a Place With Mixed Uses

Downtown is one of the few parts of Apex where a homeowner, a restaurant and an office can share a block, so the calls here are less uniform than in a subdivision. A house on a side street might need a water heater replaced. A storefront on Salem Street might need a floor drain cleared or a leak traced behind a wall.

We do not assume anything about the pipes in an older building. A structure that has been renovated more than once can contain work from several different periods, so the useful first step is finding out what is actually installed before recommending a repair or a replacement. That is also why a short description of the building's age and past work helps when you call.

For businesses, the commercial plumbing page covers the kinds of work we handle for shops, restaurants and offices. For homes, the services below are the ones most often relevant to older streets with smaller lots.

Before You Call About a Downtown Building

  • Note whether the space is a home, a business or both, and who is responsible for the plumbing, the owner or a tenant.
  • Find the main water shutoff if you can. In an older building it may be inside a utility area or near the meter, and a shared building may have more than one.
  • Take photos of any staining, dripping or damaged fixtures so the technician can see how it has changed.
  • Mention past renovations, even if you are not sure what was done. It helps narrow down what to expect.

Scheduling a Visit Downtown

Downtown sits close to the center of Apex, and Haddon Hall and Salem are both a short distance away, so technicians are often already working nearby. Non-emergency visits can usually be booked at a time that avoids event days and busy lunch hours when parking is tight.

If water is actively escaping, close the nearest shutoff first and then call the number below. Describe the building type and the street, and dispatch will take it from there.
