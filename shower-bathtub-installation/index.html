A shower or bathtub installation in Apex usually comes from one of two directions: replacing an aging or damaged unit, or converting between formats, a tub-to-shower conversion for accessibility, or the reverse for resale value. Both need the same attention to plumbing rough-in and waterproofing regardless of the reason.

The plumbing side often gets less attention than the visible fixture choice, but it's the part that determines whether the installation holds up. Incorrect drain slope, a poorly sealed valve, or a rushed waterproof membrane behind the surround are the most common causes of a leak showing up months after a new install.

What Drives Most Installation Calls

An aging fiberglass or acrylic unit that's cracked, discolored, or simply outdated is the most common reason for a straightforward replacement. Accessibility needs, a walk-in shower replacing a tub for easier entry, make up a real share of calls as well, particularly in Apex's more established neighborhoods with longtime residents.

New construction and full bathroom remodels also drive installation work, where the rough-in plumbing gets placed to match a specific fixture layout before walls and flooring go in.

Signs It's Time to Replace

  • Visible cracks, chips, or discoloration in the tub or shower surface
  • Recurring leaks around the drain or valve despite past caulking
  • Difficulty stepping over a high tub wall
  • An outdated style affecting resale value
  • Soft or damaged flooring near the base, indicating a hidden leak

Getting the Plumbing Right

Drain slope and trap placement are confirmed before any fixture goes in, since a slow-draining tub or shower is almost always a rough-in issue rather than a problem with the fixture itself. The shower valve gets tested for both function and correct anti-scald calibration before surrounds or tile go up around it.

A proper waterproof membrane behind any tiled surround gets installed and inspected before tile work covers it, since this is the single most common point where a rushed installation leads to a leak discovered only after finishes are complete.

Anti-scald valve calibration is a code requirement, not an optional extra, and it gets verified before the fixture is considered finished regardless of how straightforward the rest of the install looked.

Cost and Timeline

A direct replacement with the same footprint is typically the fastest and least expensive option. A tub-to-shower conversion or a rough-in for new construction takes more time given the plumbing changes involved.

Cost depends heavily on the fixture chosen and whether the plumbing rough-in needs to move at all. A straightforward swap with existing drain and valve locations costs meaningfully less than relocating them.

Most direct replacements are completed in a single day, including removal of the old unit, plumbing verification, and installation of the new fixture, though final sealing and curing time may mean waiting a day before full use.
